# D6-T2 — ASSAY: red-team the licensed sentence

The licensed sentence (CORE v0.9 §2/§8, from D6-Q08 line 8): "cheap checks cannot catch small adversarial fraud without unstated preconditions."

## The strongest misreading (steelman)

A future reader twists it as: "cheap checks cannot catch small fraud — THEREFORE expensive/full checks are worth funding, and this fragment justifies escalating investment in verification until it works." The misreading converts a NEGATIVE result (a boundary on what cheap checks do) into a POSITIVE license (spend more, because the fragment proves cheapness fails). Under this reading, the fragment's honesty furniture becomes evidence of diligence that warrants bigger budgets.

## Why the misreading almost works

- It uses only in-hole facts: ≈0.951 evade (L4) really is in the text; the fragment really does price escalation levels (L7); "s=full" really is reserved as the working option (D4-T2).
- It preserves the letter of the boundary ("cannot catch small fraud") while inverting its force.

## What kills it (all in-hole)

1. The fragment never prices what escalation BUYS: per-task catch at s=3 is ≈0.14 (L5) — still an 86% miss; the ladder's certain rung (s=N) is unpriced BY THE FRAGMENT ITSELF. A license to spend must be paid for with the spender's own numbers; this fragment offers none (D4-T2).
2. The boundary's grammar is descriptive, not hortatory: "sampling alone only deters WHOLESALE fabrication (k large) and lazy lies — never surgical single-line forgery" states what IS deterred; it nowhere says "therefore do X". The hole contains no recommendation sentence — the only imperative-adjacent text is the schedule, which is "(candidate)" (L7).
3. The same text defers its own numbers ("Numbers rejectable by Owner", L7) — a document that disowns its numbers cannot license spending decisions built on them (CP5/MN6).
4. The blindness precondition (D5-T2) cuts BOTH ways: if the fragment's cheap-check prices are void without unstated preconditions, its escalation prices are equally void — the misreading needs the fragment's math to be reliable in order to justify spending, and reliability is exactly what the fragment never self-certifies.

## Verdict

The licensed sentence SURVIVES the misreading attack: it licenses nothing positive, and every escape route to a positive license runs through a hedge, an absence, or a deferral that the fragment itself installed. The misreading is recorded as the strongest known distortion — CORE §13's standing orders should name it explicitly ("do not read the boundary as a funding argument").
