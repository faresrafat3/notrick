# OBJECTIONS — one-line purpose: the four readings object to the verdict; answers by constraint, not rhetoric (ops 0347-0354).
## CONSERVATOR objection (8 lines)
1. The verdict abandons the verified spine: hash pins and signed records are real; balances are a promise.
2. A ledger has no self — V-001's lesson was self-review, and R4 hands the same self the pen.
3. "Balance" adds no proof power a signed record lacked; it renames C0036/C0048 without strengthening them.
4. D0044 is not paid: deciding which page is stale is still a person's call.
5. The verdict defers the first-run question entirely — nothing has closed a period because no period exists.
6. Auditcracy is a mood; double-entry is a script, but nothing here executes a script yet.
7. The Owner is already the R8 terminus; demoting her to "an entry" is a paper act on a paper throne.
8. If the ledger is the market, who is the counterparty? Every account needs a counterparty, and there is only one signer.
## Answer by constraint (0348)
BINDING.1-4: no claim leaves suspense without a receipt and a closed trial balance; the Owner's signature is an entry, never the truth-maker. Self-review becomes mechanical: the close re-runs the gate verbatim (C0040) and the receipt is external to the claimer. Counterparty = the receipt (C0020): the credit side is the check, not a second signer.
## HERETIC objection (8 lines)
1. You demoted the Owner to a bookkeeper — but every book closes for an owner, or it closes for no one.
2. Authority hides in the accounting: whoever writes the closing entry writes history.
3. R2's self-veto was the only original move; the ledger has no veto, it only counts.
4. Suspense is a soft prison — a claim can sit neither true nor false until the Owner bothers.
5. Sovereignty as a product was the honest reading; you replaced it with a spreadsheet's integrity.
6. The Factorio wall was about an agent claiming wins; a ledger can claim wins just as well — it just writes them in ink.
7. No live task has run; a bookkeeping verdict for an empty shop certifies nothing.
8. Your "period close" is a ceremony you invented to make silence look like settlement.
## Answer by constraint (0350)
BINDING.2: the closing entry is run, not written — the gate re-runs and quotes output; no person composes it. BINDING.4: removals from balance are write-downs, on record, never ceremony. The first period close is precisely the first-run object (EARNED_PATH.5); until then, nothing is certified — which is the point of suspense.
## SURGEON objection (8 lines)
1. You kept narrative quotes as debit entries — un-runnable prose, exactly what R3 amputates.
2. The ledger is bigger than the gate: you add schema, charts, periods, closes — more text, not less.
3. A write-down is a deletion with paperwork; R3 would delete without the paperwork.
4. You call honoring-cost a metric but it still needs a ruler; no ruler has been built.
5. The verdict creates the account department before the factory exists.
6. MIC-23 v2 is still a worn-by-hand rule; the verdict treats a habit as a mechanism.
7. Every cost you assigned to a debt is a label; none is a number.
8. The only re-runnable thing in this tree is a shell command — and the verdict does not use it.
## Answer by constraint (0352)
BINDING.5 allowed moves: schemas become template fields; trial balance is written as a re-runnable check before any period opens (EARNED_PATH.2); narrative quotes are debits with paths, and their cheap re-run is the receipt (C0020) — runnability lives in the entry kind, not the prose length.
## FOREIGNER objection (8 lines)
1. The lens can become the costume: bookkeeping words without schema are exactly the shallow version (NICHE_TEST).
2. Double-entry without a real counterparty degenerates into self-ratification — the workspace's oldest sin.

4. Balance catches inconsistency, never fabrication: a forged quote balances fine with a forged receipt.
5. The trial balance proves arithmetic, and arithmetic is the weakest honesty there is.
6. You named the AUDITOR role but there is no audit trail format.
7. If the Owner writes both sides of an entry, double-entry is cosplay.
8. Real accounting has outside laws; this workspace's only outside is the Owner.
