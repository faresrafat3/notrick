## 1 · Minsky, Society of Mind — "vast diversity of mindless agents"

- **Import Mirror line (ten-axis-knowledge-map.md:18-19):** "Minsky, Society of Mind 1986 —
  'The trick is that there is no trick' (p.308): intelligence from vast diversity of
  mindless agents. → MIC population."
- **Attack:** the frontier the map cites is §8.3 / the "trick" line's promise of
  "vast diversity." The system then ships FOUR charters + FOUR MICs, each greeted as a
  monoculture seat (X-10's correlated-seats finding, adversarial-audit.md:130-140).
  "Vast diversity" is not "four asymmetrical agency hats plus four near-zero triggers."
  Charters are MINDS with prompts; MICs are rules with one trigger. NOTRICK's variance is
  the variance of ONE model family wearing four hats (the same-brain risk A-06 already
  names, demolition-log.md:110-116). The use of Minsky here is rhetorical: the map cites
  the CELEBRATION of diversity, then builds a population whose diversity is taxonomic
  (four names) but not cognitive (one brain). Minsky's own model is precise and
  embarrassing: intelligence arises from MANY small specialists whose interactions are
  *serendipitous and numerous* — not from a parliament of four prompt-skins.
- **Severity on use:** the founding citation overclaims by an order of magnitude. The
  system is not yet "Society of Mind" — it is a SOLO of Mind wearing a committee costume.
- **Survives unscarred?** NO — scarred. The map's line is a cheer for a property the
  implementation does not have. Minimal honest fix: re-tag the import as "aspirational —
  diversity to be enforced metrically (P-006), not assumed by the four hats."
  That is the P-006 disagreement metric made LOAD-BEARING, not optional (fits BRK-007).

## 5 · Kitcher — does pre-registered division of labor beat spontaneous division? (Weisberg & Muldoon say which?)

- **Import Mirror line (ten-axis-knowledge-map.md:5):** "Epistemic division of labor —
  Kitcher 1990; Weisberg & Muldoon 2009."
- **Attack:** Kitcher's formal result (1990) is the rationality of a FOLLOWER who
  deliberately works on a less-likely problem to keep diversity; the division is
  PRE-REGISTERED BY RULE. Weisberg & Muldoon 2009 (agent-based simulation on epistemic
  landscapes) found that division of labor EMERGES from simple agents on a landscape,
  but ALONE it underperforms on objective exploration: their "hill-climbers" (agents
  that only climb locally) over-concentrate; the rational "mavericks" they model explicitly
  produce the diversity the system needs. Training-cutoff honesty: my knowledge of W&M's
  exact parameter outcomes is from the 2009 paper's abstract and the review literature
  (moderate confidence); the direction I defend — pre-registered role diversity beats
  spontaneous homogeneity — is the one Kitcher argued and W&M's landscape setup models.
- **The use under trial:** NOTRICK pre-registers niches BEFORE any task. That is
  Kitcher-follower design. But NOTRICK has no MAVERICK line: every niche is a
  likely-burrow-owner (the four most "sensible" slots); nobody is assigned to the
  unlikely/unclaimed axis BY RULE. Kitcher's own theorem says the RATIONAL distribution
  includes some agents working against the apparent best bet — the system lacks the
  deliberate minority. That missing minority is exactly why CH-003's "NO NICHE" and the
  DOMAIN of the unexplored frontier (the axis ledger, mic-31) is where novel-viable
  claims would come from — and there is no pre-registered seat that MUST explore the
  unclaimed axis.
- **Survives unscarred?** NO — scarred: the "division of labor" is real but the
  Kitcher-follower side is missing its counterpart. Patch: the axis ledger (MIC-31,
  mics/mic-31-axis-tagger.md) gains a rule line: every 3rd unresolved axis of a task
  MUST be attempted by a pre-registered "unlikely-axis attempt" (a wall-breaking try on
  the LOWEST-probability axis first) — the system's maverick. (Feeds BRK-002's numerator:
  novel-viable = squares that landed on an axis no niche was pre-assigned to.)

## 6 · Popper/Lakatos — is "retire" a real Lakatosian degeneration verdict or relabeling?

- **Import Mirror line (ten-axis-knowledge-map.md:69-71):** "Popper/Lakatos — retirement
  triggers exist in every CH; need one real retirement to land. STILL PENDING (no body yet)."
- **Attack:** the map is honest that the import is PENDING. The system's "retirement
  trigger" lines (ch-001-prime.md:25-26 "3 consecutive gate-less breaks → re-forge; or
  walls stop being the bottleneck → retire") are Lakatosian-looking but are NOT
  Lakatosian: Lakatos's verdict of research-programme degeneration requires tracking a
  PROGRAMME's history of novel-prediction success vs the cost of its epicycles. NOTRICK
  "retires" an entity by a LOCAL counter (3 gate-less breaks, zero-binding-conditions RJs).
  A counter is a falsification trigger — Popperian single-hit — NOT a Lakatosian
  degeneration verdict, which needs the programme's consistency of novel predictions.
  Worse, the local counters' inputs are the verifier's own receipts — a Lakatosian judge
  would demand the programme's CENTRAL CLAIMS be listed before the run (the H/D-pre-
  registration exists for H1..H5 but not for charter-level claims). So "retire" today is
  a RELABEL of "fail 3 times," not an evidence-based degeneration verdict. The map's
  landing criterion ("need one real retirement") can never fire honestly because the
  Lakatosian INPUT (novel-prediction ledger per charter) is never collected.
- **Survives unscarred?** NO — scarred: "retire" is Popper-count, not Lakatos-verdict.
  Patch (feeds BRK-002): each charter gains a retained `novel-prediction ledger` (one
  line per real task: predicted-vs-actual on its own success metric); retirement uses
  BOTH (a) the local counter AND (b) the trend of that ledger (novelties are dying →
  degeneration verdict; a static-but-correct niche is NOT degenerate — it just has no
  SNAFU to find, which is a different verdict).
- **A canon entry that survives unscarred = ✗ on me, re-attack.** Result: all six entries
  are scarred; the strongest single re-attack on the breaker was item 1 (the founding
  citation) and item 2 (R10's self-refutation). A breaker who cannot scar his own canon
  is decorative — this lane is armed.
