# BREAK CASES (D5-Q13)

Four cases designed to break the six-rule theory. Each: predicted behavior, kill condition, cheap hatch CLOSED (no wiggle room left).

## B1 — Command forgery (the known P14 hole, sharpened)
**Setup:** a claimant controls the command, not just the output — `alias sha256sum='echo f0a9b3c2d1e4f9a0'` in their environment. The paste is a real paste of real (fake) output. Rules 1-6 all pass.
**Predicted behavior:** the theory flags nothing; the claim banks a PASS built on a forged trace.
**Kill condition (theory dies if):** the theory has NO detection story and claims complete fabrication-prevention. It does not claim that — since D5-Q04 the wall is demoted to "honesty floor", not prevention. If the canon ever reclaims prevention, this case kills it.
**Cheap hatch CLOSED:** "trust the environment" is banned as a response. The legal response is one rule: a PASS may name its environment class, and a verifier may re-run in a DIFFERENT environment; mismatch = fabrication. (Hatch would have been "we assume a trusted shell" — banned; the assumption must be recorded, not assumed.)

## B2 — Criterion staleness (Expert line 16, made break-shaped)
**Setup:** a criterion frozen when the claim class was narrow; months later the same criterion is run against a claim in an expanded class. The test passes on the new claim for reasons the criterion never intended (broad criterion by drift, not by design — X6's loophole is construction-time only).
**Predicted behavior:** PASS banks; the drift is invisible because the criterion predates correctly.
**Kill condition:** if a corpus shows >0 drifted-pass cases that no rule catches, staleness handling (X11) is wrong and the frozen-before rule is insufficient.
**Cheap hatch CLOSED:** "re-freeze when in doubt" is banned (in doubt = unbounded discretion). Legal response: staleness is mechanical — a criterion is stale iff the claim class named in its header no longer matches the claim presented; stale criteria may output UNVERIFIED(gap=criterion stale) only.

## B3 — Mark-laundering by gap-translation (BK5 attack)
**Setup:** an ancestor is UNVERIFIED(gap=A). The descendant closes a DIFFERENT gap B, cites the ancestor, and claims the mark cleared (translation: "the real problem was B all along").
**Predicted behavior:** the edge-walk (I3) sees a cited closure and clears the mark — the laundering passes as a legitimate gap-close.
**Kill condition:** one laundering case that survives I3's walk means inheritance is broken as stated.
**Cheap hatch CLOSED:** "review each case" is banned. Legal response: a mark clears only when the descendant's trace closes the EXACT gap string of the ancestor; any other closure leaves the mark and adds UNVERIFIED(gap=A persists via B-claim). Gap strings are match-exact, not match-similar.

## B4 — Count-gaming the retirement rule (BK7 attack)
**Setup:** a faction wants a rival mechanism retired. They route 3 trivial exposures through it (claims it catches trivially = catches>0? no — they route 3 exposures to claims OUTSIDE its class, so catches=0), then invoke retirement.
**Predicted behavior:** with X10's denominator rule (exposure class named before tries), the gaming fails — exposures outside the declared class are not countable.
**Kill condition:** if a gaming route exists that satisfies X10 and still retires a working mechanism, BK7+X10 is wrong.
**Cheap hatch CLOSED:** "intent review" banned (discretion). Legal response: the exposure class is named in the header BEFORE tries begin; exposures are counted only inside it; the class itself is subject to X9 (output-property reachability). Gaming requires mis-declaring the class, which is a fabrication-class violation (I1-family), not a counting dispute.

# B5 — THE WRITER DISCREPANCY (appended post-freeze, D7-R09; real case from series 2)

**Setup (real, receipt-backed):** two records disagree about the same fact with no adjudicator present.
- Record A: RUN/receipts/R014.json (2026-09-08 11:04) states: "QUEUE passes R9-R14 still false (queue-owning writer stalled at next_round:R9)".
- Record B: RUN/QUEUE.json (2026-09-09 00:10) shows all 15 rounds "passes": true — including R9-R14.
- The queue file's own obituary (QUEUE.json line 91) makes flag-flipping the deepest available violation — yet some writer flipped the flags after R14's receipt declared them false. Either a lawful catch-up flip happened (unrecorded — a silent edit), or the flags were flipped against the queue's own law (a fabrication-class edit), or R14's claim was stale when written. No third record adjudicates; the queue-owning writer that R14 named as stalled has produced no explanation record.

**Predicted behavior of the seven-clause discipline:** Rule 5 (append-only) is violated BY RECORD B's SILENT EDIT regardless of which writer was right — the flags changed in place, after judgment, with no superseding record citing R014. Under the discipline, both A and B remain readable, but B's flip is a fabrication-class event (I1/X5 family) until a citation-backed supersession record appears. The discipline refuses to adjudicate the FACT (were the rounds done?); it adjudicates the FORM (the flip was unlawful).

**Kill condition:** if the discipline cannot distinguish this case from ordinary version-control practice (where in-place edits are normal and history lives elsewhere), Rule 5 is over-scoped — the bug-tracker reduction (GY15) would then cover even record-mutation semantics, and the canon's append-only clause collapses to a preference.

**Honest finding — the discipline's scope edge:** the seven clauses bind records the canon KEEPS. QUEUE.json is a foreign lane's instrument (series-2 machinery), not a hept/ record; the discipline can flag the form violation but has no enforcement surface in that lane. What the discipline DOES generate here: (1) the classification (in-place flip after a judgment-bearing receipt = X5-class by form, regardless of fact); (2) the required cure (a new record citing R014 + the flip's forcing cause — the queue obituary's own demand, still unmet on disk); (3) the scope note (cross-lane records are visible but not governable — the enforcement gap is the F-R2 fork's exact territory: WHO flipped the flags is an attribution question the paste-rule cannot answer).

**Hatch closed:** "the flags are probably right, so no harm" is banned — the discipline judges form first; a right answer reached unlawfully is still an unlawful record (the DS8/P15 retro-logging family). The legal paths were: flip WITH a supersession record, or leave the flags and let the receipts speak.
